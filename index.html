Why a new platform

Existing tools aren't built for design strategy.

Design strategy is a small, fragmented field. Generic mentorship platforms either flood students with the wrong mentors or never connect them at all.

LinkedIn
Cold connections rarely accepted. Messages ignored. No mechanism for asynchronous mentorship.
WhatsApp groups
Noise drowns signal. Same 3 voices answer everything. Knowledge dies in scroll-back.
ADPlist
Availability gated by mentor calendars. Generic UX/PM mentors over-represented. Slow time-to-answer.
Strata
Asynchronous, AI-aggregated answers from design strategy specialists. Optional deeper 1:1 connection.
The Problem

Two groups who want to talk to each other — and can't.

Design strategy sits in the cracks between consulting, UX, product, and innovation. Both sides feel it: students don't know where they fit, and the people who could tell them don't have a frictionless way to share.

Group A · Students & early career
"I don't know what I'm graduating into."
People pursuing a Masters in Design Strategy graduate into a field that doesn't have a clear job ladder. They're unsure what roles to apply for, how to translate their past experience (architecture, product, industrial design, marketing) into strategy language, and which companies actually hire for this work.
"I have 4 years in industrial design. Recruiters either think I'm too senior or not strategic enough. I have no idea which is true." — IIT IIT-ID '25
"Is 'Innovation Strategist' even a real job, or just a title?" — Parsons MFA '26
Group B · Alumni & experienced practitioners
"I want to help — I just don't know how, or when."
Senior strategists genuinely want to support people coming up behind them. The blockers aren't generosity — they're time, decision fatigue, and not knowing what people are actually struggling with. Booking calls feels like overhead. DMs go unanswered because answering one well takes 20 minutes.
"I get the same five questions from students. I'd happily answer them once — but every conversation starts from zero." — Strategy Director, frog
"ADPlist sessions are great but I can only do one a month. There has to be a way to help more people with less time." — Founding Strategist

What we're really solving

It's not a matchmaking problem. It's a knowledge asymmetry problem with a coordination tax on top. Strata reduces both: senior practitioners contribute knowledge once in low-effort formats; AI surfaces the relevant fragments to each student instantly, with the option to escalate to a real conversation only when both sides want one.

How it works

Three steps, two roles, one feedback loop.

Strata is built around a virtuous cycle: students ask, AI surfaces answers from contributing practitioners, students rate, and the system gets better at routing the next question.

01
Practitioners drop knowledge once
Senior strategists answer short structured prompts ("What's the most common mistake you see in design strategy portfolios?") on their own time. Each contribution takes 2-3 minutes. Their answers get tagged, embedded, and credited.
02
Students ask in plain language
A student types a question — vague, specific, awkwardly phrased, it doesn't matter. Strata's AI parses intent, retrieves the most relevant practitioner answers, and synthesizes a response with citations.
03
Real names, optional follow-up
Every claim links back to the practitioner who said it. If they've marked themselves "open to connect," the student can request a 15-min async voice exchange or a scheduled call.

Why two agents instead of one

Students need to be welcomed and personalized. Practitioners need to be vetted carefully. Conflating those goals would either gatekeep students or dilute mentor quality. Atlas optimizes for activation; Vera optimizes for signal.

Atlas · Student agent
Soft, conversational. Goal is to understand goals fast, offer the right pricing, and pre-curate the platform so the first session feels useful.
Vera · Practitioner agent
Rigorous, evidence-driven. Examines answer specificity, LinkedIn history, public work. Honest "not yet" with a clear path is more respectful than a fake yes.
Both · LinkedIn verification
No anonymous accounts. Every claim links back to a real identity. This is what makes student trust and practitioner attribution possible.
Together · the trust layer
Asymmetric vetting on both sides protects each side from the other's worst case — and is the reason Strata can scale without losing quality.

The AI layer

AI is the filter, not the voice.

Every word a student reads was written by a real practitioner. Strata's AI doesn't generate opinions — it indexes, retrieves, and assembles real human knowledge so the right answer reaches the right person quickly.

The four layers

1
Ingestion
Structured prompts collect short-form practitioner answers
2
Tag & embed
LLM categorizes by role, seniority, geo, topic + vector embeds
3
Retrieve & synthesize
RAG pipeline pulls top-k answers, attribution preserved
4
Rank & route
Student feedback signals re-weight practitioner authority
01
Attribution is non-negotiable
Every sentence in a synthesized answer links back to the original practitioner contribution. Students see who said what. Practitioners get credit for every student they reach.
02
No hallucinated mentorship
The AI cannot invent advice. If no practitioner has answered a topic, the system surfaces that gap to relevant practitioners as a contribution prompt — turning a missed answer into incoming knowledge.
03
Feedback compounds
"Was this helpful?" ratings, follow-up questions, and connection requests all flow back into ranking. The best practitioners surface more. Stale or generic answers fade.
04
Knowledge gaps drive demand
When 17 students ask a variation of the same unanswered question, the system pings 3 likely-qualified practitioners with a 2-minute prompt. Demand → supply loop.

Go-to-market

Three phases, each unlocking the next.

Design strategy is small enough to seed by hand. The first 200 practitioners and 1,000 students create the gravitational pull for everyone after.

Phase 1 · 0–6 months
Seed the supply side
Recruit 100 senior practitioners via direct outreach (LinkedIn, alumni networks of top programs). Pay nothing in cash — give them attribution, recognition, and beta-shaper status. Build a corpus of ~1,500 answers across the 40 most-asked questions.
Phase 2 · 6–18 months
Activate cohorts
Partner with 3-5 masters programs (IIT-ID, Parsons, RCA, CIID, IIAD). Free Strata Plus for the cohort in exchange for being design partners. Use placement outcomes as case studies. Begin paid student conversion.
Phase 3 · 18+ months
Expand adjacent
Open to service design, innovation, and strategic UX students. Add employer features (anonymous talent surfacing, role posting). Begin India + EU + US regional sub-communities. Knowledge corpus becomes the moat.
